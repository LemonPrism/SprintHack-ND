🔐 Sentence Cipher

Turns a message into an ordinary-looking paragraph of sentences, and turns that paragraph back into the message. No passphrase, no key exchange — the recipient just needs this same page. That also means it's not confidential: anyone with this page can decode anything encoded by it. It hides the content from a casual glance or an automated scanner, not from anyone who has this tool.

How it works: your text's raw bytes become a bit stream. A couple of bits at a time pick one of four sentence shapes (3, 4, or 5 words, different word orders), then more bits fill in words from four 64-word everyday-life lists (subject / verb / object / time) — producing a mix of sentence shapes like "Mom texted dinner tonight" and "Tonight mom texted dinner again" rather than one rigid repeating pattern. Decoding reverses the lookup, using each sentence's length and small marker words ("before", "again") to tell which shape was used. There's no key and no encryption — the word lists and templates are built into this page, so anyone with a copy of it can decode anything it produces. Treat this as a format-disguise, not a way to keep something confidential. Everything runs locally in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere.
